## Calendar

| CLASS # | TOPICS |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Class Introduction <br> Course description, social determinants of health, rapid HIA client introduction. |
| 2 | Major Health Challenges from a Public Health Perspective <br> Synopsis of mortality and morbidity domestically and abroad; contrasting perspectives on health and disease. |
| 3 | Rapid HIA, Training <br> Guest lecturer: <br> [Barry Keppard, MAPC](https://www.mapc.org/staff-member/barry-keppard/) |
| 4 | Health Disparities <br> Measurement, definition, monitoring and tracking; inequity versus inequality; arguments for and against a focus on narrowing gaps in health status. |
| 5 | Socioeconomic Status <br> Income: What aspects of health can buy money? Material factors, relative position, psychosocial factors, stress, bandwidth, risk aversion. Employment: Job demand and control, job insecurity, occupational health and safety, prestige. Education: Contemporaneous and life course effects, links to other social factors. |
| 6 | HIA: Assessment Introduction |
| 7 | Discrimination, Race / Ethnicity <br> Scientific and social views on race, Latino health paradox, immigration and assimilation, discrimination, unequal returns on investment. |
| 8 | Criminal Justice System |
| 9 | Neighborhoods: Built and Natural Environment <br> Guest lecturer: <br> [Peter James](https://www.hsph.harvard.edu/news/press-releases/plants-death-rates-women/) <br> , Harvard School of Public Health <br> Air quality, water, green space, home-based hazards, heat, extreme weather/storms, climate change. Sprawl, food environment, neighborhood amenities, safety, identity and attachment. |
| 10 | Neighborhoods: Social Environment <br> What is a “neighborhood effect?” Community change, displacement, social capital. |
| 11 | Embodiment <br> Physiological effects of stress, immune function, life course perspective, intergenerational transmission. |
| 12 | Policy and Policymaking <br> Guest lecturer: <br> [Lauren Smith, FSG](http://www.fsg.org/people/lauren-a-smith) <br> (former Commissioner, Massachusetts Department of Public Health) <br> Costs and benefits of bringing a health lens to non-health policies; planning and the Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act. |
| 13 | Pathway presentations |
| 14 | HIA: Applications <br> Reviewing examples of HIAs, HIA process, scoping working session. |
| 15 | HIA: Screening and Scoping <br> Guest lecturer: Barry Keppard, MAPC <br> Picking a policy, program, budget, plan for assessment; prioritizing health risks and health outcomes to examine. |
| 16 | HIA Assessment Workshop: Record Expungement |
| 17 | HIA Assessment Workshop: Just Cause for Eviction |
| 18 | HIA: Designing Recommendations <br> Using evidence effectively. <br> HIA Critique Due |
| 19 | Working Session <br> Informal class presentations, time to workshop in teams. |
| 20 | HIA: Successes and Failures <br> HIA pitfalls and triumphs, with examples. |
| 21 | State of the Practice <br> Guest lecturer: Bethany Rogerson, <br> [Health Impact Project](http://www.pewtrusts.org/en/projects/health-impact-project/about) <br> The breadth, scope, and growth of HIA practice. Comparison to other impact assessment. |
| 22 | Alternatives to HIA <br> Guest lecturer: <br> [Barry Keppard, MAPC](http://www.mapc.org/about-mapc/staff/barry-keppard-0) <br> Health in all policies, health lens analysis, other tools. |
| 23 | HIA: Communications <br> Framing, organizing, advocacy. |
| 24 | Final HIA presentations with clients |
| 25 | Final HIA presentations with clients (Cont.) |
| 26 | Future of HIA <br> Where do we go from here? Will HIA continue to spread? Should it? |
