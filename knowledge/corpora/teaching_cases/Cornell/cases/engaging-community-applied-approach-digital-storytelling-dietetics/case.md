## Context

"Two of my courses have community-engaged learning components, and I work with a variety of community partners. Several partners (Cornell Dining, Cornell Sports, Southern Tier Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics) mainly engage with their participants via social media and requested digital storytelling/social media as deliverables. Also, as a former community registered dietitian, I was often called upon to contribute to my organization's social media platforms, and I knew this was a skill set my students needed to develop.

Both courses focus on creating evidence-based, client-facing nutrition education. In the past, this would have been delivered as a handout, poster or in-person presentation, but sadly, all of these formats are becoming obsolete. I realized that students are great consumers but inexperienced as producers of quality social media content. Working with CTI has enhanced my ability to give dietetic and nutrition students cutting-edge skills they will need as they enter the workforce."

Kelly Quinn, Lecturer of Nutritional Sciences

## Implementation

In the early stages of developing this assignment, Quinn requested a consultation with CTI’s Storylab, part of the [Creative Technology Lab](https://teaching.cornell.edu/learning-technologies/creative-technology-lab). Together, Quinn and CTI instructional designers developed a rubric and assignment activities for the students to follow. Once the semester started, a member of Storylab’s staff visited Quinn’s class twice. During the first visit, instructional designers discussed best practices of creating videos when working with community partners. They also discussed best practices for developing videos including planning, script creation, collecting media and editing. The second involved a technology demonstration of video editing tools.

## Challenges

- **Skills building:** Despite being digital natives, students do not necessarily have the technical skills necessary to create a well-developed and edited video/podcast, and may need structured activities to help them develop their projects.
- **Time management:** Digital storytelling assignments typically take longer than a traditional paper or presentation to create, and at the same time, students often do not believe creating a video or podcast will be a time-intensive project.
- **Lesson planning:** Consider whether you will devote class time to workshop scripts and/or story ideas?
- **Design & evaluation:**Consider whether this assignment will be a group project or each individual will have to create a video/podcast/storymap, and how you will evaluate this assignment accordingly.

## Reflection and Future Directions

Quinn plans to continue partnering with CTI instructional designers and using these assignments as community partners typically desire digital content to assist with their communications and messaging priorities.

## How to Adapt This for Your Class

![image](https://teaching.cornell.edu/sites/default/files/2025-09/adapt%20icon%402x.png)

Depending on how you structure the assignment, having students create a video or podcast can help them practice communication and writing skills, as well as working in groups and even project planning.

### Preparation

- Define the assignment goals.
- Find examples of successful and unsuccessful content to show students.
- Decide how you will grade the assignments & develop rubrics.
- Decide whether this will be an individual or group assignment.

### Assignment

- Clearly state the assignment goals and steps.
- Consider grading each step of the assignment. For instance, separately evaluating different assignment components, such as an annotated bibliography, the script, the collected media and the final video/podcast, can help students invest in the creation process and develop their projects over time. Only grading the final product often results in a poorly executed final product.
- Introduce examples of successful and unsuccessful content and analyze them with your students.

### Evaluation

- Follow a rubric. Consider sharing the rubric with your students in advance, so they know what to expect, and how to succeed.
- Host an in-class watching/listening session with peer evaluation.
- Ask students to reflect on and discuss transferable skills.
