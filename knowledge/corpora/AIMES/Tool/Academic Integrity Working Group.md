**The AIWG is charged with addressing a variety of issues related to academic integrity at Stanford University.** It aims to study and understand the causes of academic dishonesty in contemporary settings and to assess the interplay of academic integrity and pedagogical practices both here and elsewhere. It will ultimately recommend policies and other measures to address these issues.

## The Status of Proctoring at Stanford

Beginning September 1, 2026, proctoring of in-person assessments will be permitted university-wide.

- **April 26, 2026 Update:** The Board on Conduct Affairs, the Undergraduate Senate, the Graduate Student Council, the Senate of the Academic Council, and the Office of the President have all voted to allow proctoring of in-person exams following the pilot overseen by the AIWG. **Beginning Fall 2026, "*****To foster a climate of academic honesty, effective learning, and fair assessment, instructors are permitted to proctor any in-person assessments.*****"**
- **May 22nd, 2026 Update:** The Board on Conduct Affairs (BCA) has adopted the [Stanford Proctoring Bylaw](https://communitystandards.stanford.edu/policies-guidance%23policies-guidance-links/bylaws-charter#stanford-proctoring-bylaw). The Bylaw provides a framework for fair and effective in-person proctoring.

## 2026-27 AIWG Guidance & Recommendations

### AIWG Recommendations on In-Person Exams

The BCA has adopted the [Stanford Proctoring Bylaw](https://communitystandards.stanford.edu/policies-guidance%23policies-guidance-links/bylaws-charter#stanford-proctoring-bylaw), providing a framework for fair and effective in-person proctoring. *“To foster a climate of academic honesty, effective learning, and fair assessment, instructors are permitted to proctor any in-person assessments.”* Building on this updated guidance, the AIWG strongly recommends, as much as feasible, that high-stakes assessments be moved into in-person settings and proctored in accordance with the Stanford Honor Code.

### AIWG Guidance on Smart Devices & Generative AI

- **Smart Devices During Assessments:** Smart devices, including but not limited to cell phones, tablets, smart watches, smart glasses, and other internet-capable devices, are not permitted during quizzes, exams, or other assessments unless explicitly authorized by the instructor. Devices should be turned completely off and stored away in a backpack or bag, not kept on a person's body, before assessments begin.
- **Smart Glasses:** As smart eyewear becomes more common, individuals who wear prescription smart glasses must have regular (non-technology-enabled) glasses for quizzes, exams, and other similar academic activities, as smart glasses are not permitted to be worn during assessments.
- The[AIMES website](https://ctl.stanford.edu/aimes), launched by the [Center for Teaching and Learning (CTL)](https://ctl.stanford.edu/), provides guidance and examples of how AI can be integrated into coursework in ways that support student learning and career preparation. CTL encourages departments to [reach out](https://stanforduniversity.qualtrics.com/jfe/form/SV_78KTbL61clEWsO9) for individual consultations and department level workshops.

## Fall 2026-27: Proctoring Implementation & Resource FAQs

### What changes were made to the Honor Code and Student Conduct Charter?

The [Honor Code](https://communitystandards.stanford.edu/policies-guidance/honor-code) was updated to permit the proctoring of in-person assessments beginning Fall 2026. The [Student Conduct Charter](https://communitystandards.stanford.edu/stanford-student-conduct-charter-2023) was updated to clarify the Board on Conduct Affairs' authority to provide interpretive guidance and formulate [proctoring bylaws](https://communitystandards.stanford.edu/policies-guidance%23policies-guidance-links/bylaws-charter#stanford-proctoring-bylaw). Additional information is available on the linked policy pages.

### What are the roles and responsibilities for proctoring at Stanford?

The [Stanford Proctoring Bylaw](https://communitystandards.stanford.edu/policies-guidance%23policies-guidance-links/bylaws-charter?check_logged_in=1#stanford-proctoring-bylaw), adopted by the Board on Conduct Affairs (BCA), provides the university's framework for fair and effective in-person proctoring. The bylaw outlines roles and responsibilities of instructors, students, departments, and campus partners in supporting fair and effective in-person proctoring. The bylaw should be reviewed by anyone involved in administering or participating in proctored assessments.

The AIWG resources below supplement the Proctoring Bylaw by providing implementation guidance, best practices, and supporting resources developed to assist departments and instructors as proctoring is implemented across the university.

### Do proctors need to complete training before proctoring an assessment?

Yes. Per the BCA Proctoring Bylaw, all proctors should complete the brief, self-paced online Stanford Proctor Training before proctoring assessments. Faculty, instructors, and course staff can access the [Stanford Proctor Training course through the self-enrollment link.](https://aiwg.stanford.edu/training)

### What syllabus language should I use if assessments may be proctored?

Faculty and instructors who choose to proctor assessments are strongly encouraged to include the following statement in their syllabus: *"To foster a climate of academic honesty, effective learning, and fair assessment, examinations in this course may be proctored in accordance with the Stanford Honor Code."*

In addition, we recommend that instructors communicate expectations regarding smart devices before assessments, whether through the course syllabus, assessment instructions, or other course communications. The AIWG recommends sharing the following guidance with students: "*Smart devices, including but not limited to cell phones, tablets, smart watches, smart glasses, and other internet-capable devices, are not permitted during quizzes, exams, or other assessments unless explicitly authorized by the instructor. Devices should be turned completely off and stored away in a backpack or bag, not kept on a person's body, before assessments begin."*

### Where can I find guidance on supporting students with exam accommodations and using the Centralized Testing Center?

The [Centralized Testing Center](https://oae.stanford.edu/ctc) (CTC) administers and proctors exams for students who are actively registered with the [Office of Accessible Education](https://oae.stanford.edu/) (OAE) and have approved testing accommodations. For the most current information about the CTC, including guidance and resources for students and instructors, visit the [Centralized Testing Center page](https://oae.stanford.edu/ctc).

### Where can I find guidance for student-athletes who need to take an exam while traveling or away from campus?

Stanford Athletics provides additional resources and guidance for instructors working with student-athletes, including students who may need to complete exams while traveling. Visit the [Working with Student-Athletes](https://advising.stanford.edu/faculty-and-staff/aarc-resources) resources page for more information.

### What should I do if a student will be traveling or unable to attend an in-person assessment?

Instructors should communicate expectations and available options to students in advance. Guidance for common situations, including student travel, can be found in the Traveling Students and Alternate Testing Arrangements resource. The following link provides [Tips and Resources for Off-Campus Proctoring Situations](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1C_PmMfuOn4qQmI1kjBal286Y-Q6Binzn?usp=drive_link).

### Are seating charts available for proctored exams?

Yes. Instructors may select the seating arrangement that best fits their classroom and assessment needs. Additional tips, instructions, and seating templates are available in the [**Exam Assigned Seating and Exam Break Sign-Out Sheet**](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1hacx0ISXOE-0E4JNiP1OC4bSPh_9I1R6) folder.

The [**Seat Assign Tool**](https://lts.stanford.edu/seatassign), developed by Learning Technologies & Spaces (LTS), can help instructors create assigned seating arrangements for some of the most commonly used fixed-seating exam rooms. The tool allows instructors to download seat lists with a variety of spacing options, along with a schematic map of the room and seats.

### How should breaks be managed during a proctored exam?

Break procedures are determined by the instructor and should be communicated clearly to students before the assessment. Instructors may require students to sign out and sign back in when leaving the exam room and leave their exam materials with course staff during the break. See the [Exam Break Sign Out Sheet](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1zamixwuyi5jPfBdNZwj-b_cEm7ifuhswD7AaKJ1qyNQ/edit?usp=drive_link) and implementation guidance for an example approach.

### Is online or remote proctoring permitted?

No. The [Proctoring Bylaw](https://communitystandards.stanford.edu/policies-guidance%23policies-guidance-links/bylaws-charter#stanford-proctoring-bylaw) applies only to in-person assessments. Remote proctoring, whether by software or through a human proctor connecting via the internet, is not permitted. Some courses may choose to use lockdown browsers during an in-person proctored exam if they wish to run exams online or provide students access to specific software during exams. As of Spring 2026, lockdown browser tools are in pilot testing at Stanford; for more information, instructors are encouraged to reach out to the Canvas team by email at canvashelp@stanford.edu .

### AIWG Composition Guidance

**Composition Guidance from the AIWG Charge**

“The AIWG will consist of four students (two graduate and two undergraduate, from diverse backgrounds including first-gen/low-income representation) and four faculty/lecturers (especially from departments with the most frequent instances of in-person exam cheating concerns), along with one person each from the Office of Community Standards (OCS) and Office of General Counsel (OGC). Student appointments will be for one academic year with the option to apply for renewal. Membership in the AIWG is to be determined respectively by the ASSU Nominations Commission, the Faculty Senate Committee on Committees, and the VPSA (for OCS and OGC). It will meet with stakeholders (student groups, departmental representatives, Academic Advising, etc.) to inform its proposals. Non-voting members may include representatives from the Center for Teaching and Learning, the Office of Accessible Education and others as identified by the Working Group. VPSA will provide resources for project management.”

### AIWG Proctoring Pilot

The AIWG began the multi-year pilot of equitable in-person proctoring practices at Stanford, Spring Quarter 2024. [Review the Pilot Guidelines, Courses Participating, and More](https://studentaffairs.stanford.edu/AIWG/aiwg-proctoring-pilot)

### Student Affairs & The AIWG

Student Affairs does not unilaterally direct or control the Academic Integrity Working Group (AIWG) or its processes. Instead, Student Affairs' role is to offer resources for project management and to facilitate the procurement of an unbiased external consulting group to support the proctoring pilot. Student Affairs maintains a neutral & objective position and does not attempt to influence AIWG operations.
