## Course Policy：Course AI Policy

**来源**：https://ctl.stanford.edu/aimes/aimes-library/media-mind-and-black-mirror-policy（访问 2026-10-02）

This course policy allows the usage of AI on assignments but not exams and models disclosure via a template.

## Course Info:

- COMM 173A/273A: "Media, Mind, and Black Mirror"
- Spring 2025
- Instructor: Robby Ratan

## Pedagogy:

In addition to articulating when the use of generative AI is and is not allowed in the course, the policy provides a template for disclosure with an example. Note that this course encourages students to critically analyze media technologies and their impacts. The instructor also tells students, "in line with the theme of the class, students will be encouraged to use any advanced media technology at their disposal, including generative AI tools, to develop the assignment content." Students' assignments are not only graded on the technical product, but also on the creativity, synthesis across concepts, connections to unique personal experiences, and how students speak about their ideas.

## Course AI Policy

### AI Tool Use Policy

Using Generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) Agents or Large Language Models (LLMs)—such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity, etc.—to generate assignments or parts of assignments is TOTALLY allowable on written assignments (BUT NOT ON EXAMS) as long as you disclose this use clearly. Failing to do so may qualify as a breach of the honor code and lead to an academic dishonesty issue on your record. Hence, if you use an AI or LLM to generate content, you must include an **AI Assistance Disclosure Statement**.

Here is an example/template:

This [research paper] was developed with the assistance of artificial intelligence (AI) tools, specifically [e.g., ChatGPT 4o, Gemini Advanced 1.5, etc.], in order to [generate ideas, draft sections, correct grammar and style, summarize research, format citations, etc.], but not to [generate or fabricate data, conduct analyses, or interpret results]. I, the human author, reviewed and edited the AI’s contributions to ensure accuracy, originality, and alignment with the objectives of the [research, project], and followed ethical guidelines for content attributions, academic integrity and originality. I take final responsibility for the content, arguments, and conclusions of this [paper] .*

*This AI Assistance Disclosure Statement example/template was generated with the help of ChatGPT 4o to generate a draft outline, which I then edited into this single paragraph. I take final responsibility for the content of this example/template statement.

## Assignment：Final Project

**来源**：https://ctl.stanford.edu/aimes/aimes-library/media-mind-and-black-mirror-final（访问 2026-10-02）

Students generate data for a final project by interacting with a chatbot throughout the course.

## Course Info:

- COMM 173A/273A: "Media, Mind, and Black Mirror"
- Spring 2025
- Instructor: Robby Ratan

## Pedagogy:

This final project requires ongoing work every week of the course and involves extended interaction with a large language model (LLM), generative AI chatbot. Students reflect on their interactions every week and use the full quarter-long record of their interactions with the LLM as data for their final project, which applies media psychology research methods discussed in the course.

## Final Project

*Black Mirror* depicts potential technological developments that will dramatically transform humanity. Your final project for this class will involve experimenting with one of these (now real!) technologies, an AI/LLM-based chat partner.

Specifically, you will customize and converse with your own personal AI chat partner (e.g., ChatGPT) 2-3 times a week, 10 minutes per conversation, and then complete surveys reflecting on your interactions. After a few weeks of this activity, you will write an analytical essay about your experience. You will present your main insights from the essay to the class in your final presentation, optionally working with a group of classmates.

### Part 1

Customize your chat partner—likely ChatGPT (or similar; please get approval from the teaching team)—by completing this chat partner Customization Template and pasting the text into your chat partner interface. Throughout the assignment, feel free to update your customization preferences with additional instructions (the more specific, the better).

### Part 2

Talk with your chat partner using the same thread every time (to ensure it remembers your interactions). You are encouraged to also interact with your chat partner via VOICE CHAT at least twice during this assignment (e.g., by using the ChatGPT App on your phone; [Voice FAQ here](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8400625-voice-mode-faq)).

Please try to have multiple interactions, at least 2-3 times a week, for roughly ten minutes each. You can talk about whatever you like with your chat partner, but you are also encouraged to use at least 2-3 questions from the three different templates we provide. At the end of each week (between Friday and Sunday), please complete a reflective survey about your interactions.

### Part 3

At the end of the quarter you will write up an auto-observational study (600-1000 words) based on the data you’ve collected about your own chat partner experience. You will deliver a written report that will consist of the following sections: Section 1: Describe your chat partner. Section 2: Reflect on your survey responses — read them in detail. Section 3: Connect your observations to existing research publications.Section 4: Conclusion

### Part 4

You will conclude this project by giving a two-minute presentation (one slide) summarizing your experience and your write-up. Key points to hit will be: a) How you customized your chat partner. b) The main insights from your reflections on your interacting with your chat partner from your analyses of your survey responses. c) How your observations relate to existing research publications. d) The main theoretical and practical implications.

## Assignment：Short Assignments

**来源**：https://ctl.stanford.edu/opportunities/media-mind-and-black-mirror-short（访问 2026-10-02）

AI-focused assignments are used as the class prep material for each day of this course.

## Course Info:

- COMM 173A/273A: "Media, Mind, and Black Mirror"
- Spring 2025
- Instructor: Robby Ratan

## Pedagogy:

In addition to synthesizing insights from assigned texts and media, this short assignment also supplies the material for in-class activity, and encourages students to synthesize pop culture and relevant research to propose research ideas and utilize AI as a tool for abstract creation.

The instructor also used AI in realtime as a tool to help facilitate class discussion. Using the [Stanford AI Playground](https://uit.stanford.edu/aiplayground), Ratan describes the process: "I pasted the students' reading response posts into the AI tool and asked for 1) a summary of similar themes between students, and 2) the top three most innovative/unique posts. I did this in realtime so that I could use the AI results to guide the discussion with the full class about their insights related to the material."

## Short Assignments

### Class Prep Papers

(post on Canvas by 10am on MW before each class)

After reading the relevant research article (do this first) and watching the relevant *Black Mirror* episode, please submit a short paper (300 - 500 words, NOT including the abstract) before each class with the following:

1. Scene-to-Theme Connections
 1. Choose a scene from the relevant *BM* episode that relates to a substantive theme from the relevant research article.
 2. Summarize the *BM* episode scene and the theme (1-2 sentences each). (E.g., “In San Junipero (15:26-17:55), Yorkie is ____. This relates to the Proteus effect, the phenomenon that people tend to ….”)
2. Describe how the scene relates to the theme
 1. e.g., “When Yorkie is ____, she acts in a way that ____. This reflects the Proteus effect because …”
 2. Synthesis for IRL
 1. Synthesize your scene-to-theme connection (Scene-them-size!) for modern or foreseeable-future media use, explaining implications for people psychologically or socially. (E.g., “As people increasingly use VR to ____, their avatars will likely ___.”)
 2. Briefly describe what type of research would be valuable in this area. (E.g., “Researchers should examine if people who ____ are more ____.”)
3. Research idea
 1. Propose a creative research study inspired by your previous answers. Try to propose an idea that is novel, feasible, and relevant. Explain (at a high-level) the following elements of your idea:
 1. Proposed Study Title
 2. Main research question
 3. Hypotheses or general expectations of what your study might find.
 4. Explanation of why you have those expectations
 5. Proposed method
 1. Qual (interviews, ethnography, etc.) or Quant (surveys, experiment)
 2. Sample (context, community, types of participants)
 3. Procedure (how you will collect data)
4. Abstract/summaries
 1. Use an AI/LLM tool (e.g., ChatGPT; [Stanford’s AI Playground](https://uit.stanford.edu/aiplayground)) to generate THREE separate ~50 word summaries of each section above (the abstract is NOT included in the 300-500 word limit)
 1. Connections
 2. Synthesis
 3. Research idea
 2. Edit the GenAI summaries to your liking, and paste them at the top of your document under the heading "Abstract."

##### Note

Please be prepared to discuss your idea in detail during class meetings. This will be the PRIMARY activity in our class, so please take it seriously and make sure you can support your claims with evidence.
