## In-class Activity：Group presentations:

**来源**：https://ctl.stanford.edu/aimes/aimes-library/ai-art-aesthetics（访问 2026-10-02）

These in-class activities provide a structure for students to guide their peers through the creative possibilities of AI.

## Course Info:

- ARTSTUDI/FILMEDIA 192/392: "AI Art & Aesthetics"
- Winter 2026
- Instructors: Shane Denson, Miguel Novelo

## Pedagogy:

These in-class activities in "AI Art & Aesthetics" are student-led, with the instructors providing a format for student groups to develop activities for their peers. This not only provides students with agency, but allows them to bring in their own knowledge of and questions about AI's relationship to art.

Some background: "AI Art & Aesthetics" is one of Stanford's courses that challenges students both to *use* and to *analyze the use of* generative AI. Co-taught by an artist and a media theorist, this course has two objectives: (1) introduce students to key theories and debates around AI in contemporary art, and (2) challenge students to make thoughtful, critical, and rigorous artworks of their own. The course positions AI art as a complex subject that demands equal consideration of technological, artistic, social, and philosophical concerns. It situates these elements within contemporary cultural realities and addresses critical aspects of working with AI, with a particular focus on ethical and political dimensions.

## Group presentations:

In the second week, we will create groups. Each group will research and choose an artist that works with artificial intelligence in some manner; we will provide a list of artists, but you are welcome to find others outside the list. The group should present their work and connect with a core idea previously discussed in class. Presentations should be around 10 minutes and include a short activity of 10 minutes. Activity can be sketching, performing an action, a short art practice demo, a discussion, a writing response, etc.

## In-Class Activity Example 1

Create a piece of software inspired by one or more topics discussed in the introduction of the class. Think about how your project can utilize interactive, image-based, sound-based, text-based, animation, and generative forms of making through the practice of poetic engineering.

In pairs:

- Discuss: What is art? Create a system that describes your idea.
- Create a tool utilizing an LLM to produce a piece of software.
- Create at least 3 iterations to showcase the progression.
- Come up with a project title, and write a description of your project.

Be sure to include the title, the LLM used, the main prompt, and the description. For example:

**Title:** Cave
**LLM:** Gemini 3 (Fast)
**Prompt:**"Make an html with JavaScript where every time I hover the mouse on the page, lines are drawn. Carving lines in a cave, the website should make horses, humans, cows, and dogs. Last, make sure there is fictional fire illuminating the cave."
**Description:** "This cave is the whole html canvas. This cave is reactive to the mouse; new shapes are forming every time the mouse is moved. The cave should be tridimensional. Try to come up with an illusion.”

## In-Class Activity Example 2

Create an autonomous sequence of events that create media. Utilizing Automator, Task Scheduling, or MCP to create a system that creates recursive processes to create images, videos, texts, or a series of computational performances.

This course encourages students to critically consider the role of AI in art, through ethical, political, and historical lenses.

## Pedagogy:

The major project for this course exemplifies strong practices for extended assignments, with particular emphasis on the role of AI in the process. First, the project is in itself designed to focus student attention on the relationship of human and artificial intelligence, and to provide a basis for students to critique that relationship. Second, the structure and grading of the project emphasizes process and iteration, with deep reliance on human feedback and revision. Finally, the reasons for use or avoidance of AI at each project step have been adapted to the particular needs of the assignment, and are made explicit to the students.

## Project Steps:

1. Procedural: A set of rules or process design as a key component of the work.
2. Simulation: Iteration of project 1— Create work on artificial intelligence, on simulation of life, biomimicry, synthetic, or simulacra.
3. Agency/Autonomy: Iterations of project 2 — Find a conceptual framework from the readings discussed and shared in class and evolve the project utilizing AI as a medium.

### Project Step 1

Conditions: Do not use any computational or digital media. Do not use any LLM for conceptualization or research. Avoid, at all costs, using your smartphone or computer.

1. Find an idea: Create a work about intelligence. Where is intelligence residing? What is your relationship with the concept of intelligence? Can you define intelligence from a non-human perspective? Think outside of what we have covered in class. Find research, articles, or media that discuss intelligence in non-conventional forms. Can you create a new definition of intelligence?
2. Define a system: Just like prompt engineering, define a system where the output declares, defines, instantiates, or exemplifies your idea. Remember, art itself is the system and output. Remember that we are not using digital or computational media for this project.
3. Present your system in a gallery setting. Think about the audience who will be engaging with your idea and system. Is this a performance to be replicated like Lauren McCarthy's I’m Lauren? Is this a performance like Bruce Nauman, Walking in an Exaggerated Manner Around the Perimeter of a Square? Or perhaps a system for generating line drawings like Sol Lewitt? While homage and references are key for art practice, we invite you to think beyond and explore what is a system from a personal standpoint.

### Project Step 2

Conditions: Artwork will be presented. Create slides for a 10-minute presentation.

Artwork will be presented outside of the computer, with either a separate screen/monitor, projection, print (2D or 3D) or any other form of media, but should not be displayed on a laptop. Can you replicate your work from Project 1 by prompting? How can Nano Banana reinterpret your project from an aesthetic and conceptual perspective? What’s gain, value, or transform?

For project 2, you will either try to mimic 100% of your work with artificial intelligence tools, or you can take the evolution approach and evolve your project into new conceptual spaces and ideas. Either of these processes would be satisfactory. Keep in mind that documentation would count as part of the art piece. So, think about the presentation as well. Some examples: a series of images illustrating the evolution of the work, a visual map, a vlog, an essay, a video essay, a zine, etc.

Please document all aspects of your process, what tools and how you are using the tools, software, your prompts and conversations, how artificial intelligence affects your thinking. For deliverables, each student will present for 10 minutes, demonstrating the art practice process and evolution of the work + art project.

### Project Step 3 (Final)

Pick a reading from the list on the Canvas page.

After reading one or more texts, choose at least one of them and identify conceptual frameworks and aesthetic inspirations. Next build on your previous project, utilizing the text to modify, adapt, and transform your previous project. The final project is concerned with adaptability and the evolution of your original work.

How does the conceptual framework of the text make space for new meaning in your artwork?

How does the language transform your thinking about it or your framing of it for the viewer’s encounter with it?

## Grading

Your project will be evaluated based on how well you engaged with the themes of “Simulation,” “Procedurality,” and “Agency/Autonomy,” while reflecting on methods such as "critical misuse" and how such methods support your investigation and final outputs. But most importantly, we are looking for an iterative process where you build on and deepen your ideas and project over the three-four weeks of each assignment. Successful projects will shift and change based on what you are discovering through your work and class feedback. Have fun and push yourself to try new things and explore!
