## Pedagogy:

Many instructors struggle to develop an AI course policy that will make sense from the ethical and technological perspective of students. In COMM 178A, Djordje Padejski addresses this issue by asking students to co-create the policy. In the statement below, Padejski provides the larger contexts for students to consider in developing the policy: educational ("can either enhance or impede learning"), course-level ("achieve key learning outcomes"), and institutional ("consistent with guidance from the Board of Conduct Affairs regarding use of AI and the Stanford Honor Code"). Extending trust to students in co-creation of the policy invites them to bring their standards to the table; grounding the discussion in larger contexts helps to keep students on the same page as the instructor about the ultimate goals for the course. For another example of a course using co-creation of an AI policy, see [History 200B](https://ctl.stanford.edu/opportunities/doing-environmental-history-water-justice).

## Course Policies for Use of Generative AI

In this course, artificial intelligence (AI) and tools like ChatGPT are both subjects of study and pedagogical resources. Depending on the context, artificial intelligence (AI) can either enhance and impede learning. There are times when these tools might provide us with new ideas and understandings (e.g., brainstorming or getting feedback). At other points, practicing skills and synthesizing ideas on our own will be critical for the learning process. Thus, being mindful of when to use these tools will help us navigate the complexity of interacting with AI technologies in the classroom and beyond.

In this class, we will use AI tools extensively, including ChatGPT, as part of the focus on understanding the impact of AI to journalism, AI fundamentals, ethical implications, and AI applications. Doing so will help you achieve key learning outcomes, such as developing critical thinking and analytical skills to evaluate societal impacts of AI, and preparing investigative proposals on AI systems.

At the beginning of the course, we will co-create a class agreement on the use of AI tools that ensures everyone has equal access to such tools and knowledge of their benefits and limitations; understands the appropriate use of them; and is clear on policies and procedures for their use (including proper citation), and how they connect to the idea of academic integrity. Our class agreement will be consistent with guidance from the [Board of Conduct Affairs regarding use of AI](https://communitystandards.stanford.edu/policies-guidance%23policies-guidance-links/bca-guidance-recommendations#generative-ai-policy-guidance) and the [Stanford Honor Code,](https://communitystandards.stanford.edu/policies-guidance/honor-code) which notes that use of generative AI to “substantially complete” an assignment or exam by entering the prompt and submitting the output as one’s own work is not permitted.

## Learning Goals：Course Learning Goals

The learning goals for this journalism class highlights a multidisciplinary, analytical approach to evaluating AI systems.

## Course Info:

- COMM 178A: "Artificial Intelligence and Journalism"
- Fall 2025
- Instructors: Djordje Padejski

## Pedagogy:

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is transforming every aspect of our lives, including journalism. This course offers a comprehensive understanding of the impact of AI on society and the field of journalism, prepares students to navigate the uncertainties and opportunities AI presents, and empowers them with the knowledge and skills to report on this topic. It explores AI from a multidisciplinary perspective; students engage with various investigative techniques, empowering them to scrutinize AI algorithms and their societal implications, but also use AI tools to understand how AI is reshaping journalism.

## Course Learning Goals

As AI continues to redefine journalism, this course prepares students to navigate the uncertainties and opportunities presented by these new transformative and disruptive technologies and empowers them with the knowledge and skills to report on this topic. By the end of this course students will learn how to:

- Understand the fundamentals of artificial intelligence as an interdisciplinary field, including its history, types, and applications.
- Apply critical thinking and analytical skills to evaluate the social and ethical implications of AI systems in different contexts.
- Develop an understanding of the challenges and opportunities of AI in journalism and how it can be leveraged to enhance reporting.
- Prepare a story proposal to investigate artificial intelligence technologies.
- Identify and evaluate sources of information on AI and its impacts on society, including ethical, legal, and technical aspects.

## Course Materials

There is no required textbook for this class. Selected readings will be provided through the Canvas page for the course. No prior computer programming experience or knowledge of AI is required.
