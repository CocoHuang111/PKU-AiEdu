## Pedagogy:

Professor Pan's course policy for "Communication and Propaganda" strikes a balance between openness and rigor. Her syllabus acknowledges that AI tools are becoming widely used, and allows students to use them in all work except two in-class quizzes. In addition, students are reminded of their ultimate accountability for the accuracy of all works they submit. Rather than threatening harsh penalties for the use of AI, the syllabus simply explains that uncorrected AI mistakes are taken into account in grading. Though not stated here, the same rule is applied to student writing by way of a creativity metric in the grading rubric: since AI writing often lacks for creativity, its use for creating papers is discouraged through low grading for creativity.

## Generative AI Course Policy

Generative AI tools (e.g., ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot, or other large language models) are increasingly common tools for writing, coding, and brainstorming. In this course, you may use generative AI in any part of your work except for the two in-class quizzes. Quizzes must be completed individually, without external assistance, in order to evaluate your own learning.

When you use generative AI, you take full responsibility for the accuracy, appropriateness, and originality of your work. AI outputs often contain errors, omissions, or misleading claims; it is your responsibility to verify correctness. Submissions with uncorrected AI errors will be graded accordingly.

This policy treats AI use analogously to assistance from another person: prohibited on in-class quizzes and always requiring critical evaluation.
