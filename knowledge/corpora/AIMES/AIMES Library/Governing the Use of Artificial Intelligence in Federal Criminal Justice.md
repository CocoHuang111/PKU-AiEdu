The AI policy for this law policy course discourages the usage of AI-generated content, but allows some usage for research.

## Course Info:

- LAW 810C: "Policy Practicum: Governing the Use of Artificial Intelligence in Federal Criminal Justice"
- Winter 2026
- Instructors: Jonathan Wroblewski, Robert Weisberg

## Pedagogy:

This law policy class centers on developing a framework for AI for the federal criminal justice system. For the course's own AI policy, the instructors encourages students to limit AI use to research collation, and requires a citation of the specific tool, how it was used, the prompt, and the output. This approach, which could be called "check-and-cite-if-used," is becoming standard for AI in many contexts (see, for instance, the policy for [this Stanford journalism course](https://ctl.stanford.edu/opportunities/artificial-intelligence-and-journalism-0)). It is also helpful in this example that the instructors pledge a discussion with the students on the first day of class.

## Course Policy

### Use of AI

Use of Artificial Intelligence: We will discuss this at our first class meeting. While you may use AI tools for research and other purposes, all research output should be reviewed and checked for accuracy, and we encourage you to avoid using AI at all to write memos or papers, or to generate PowerPoint and other presentations. If you choose to do so, however, you must identify the AI product you used and submit a paragraph about how you used AI, the prompts you used, and a summary of the AI output.
