## Pedagogy:

The direct, personal approach of this course policy speaks straight to the students and relates the use of AI to their educational journey. This person-to-person mode is continued through the pledge to co-develop a class code on AI with the students (see also [COMM 178A](https://ctl.stanford.edu/opportunities/artificial-intelligence-and-journalism-0)). This syllabus statement also helps students to parse the difference between using AI to improve one's work versus outsourcing one's own thinking to a bot. Another interesting feature of this example is the tie-in between course policy and class content (ie, "the AI industry's impact on water use").

## Course Policy

### Use of AI

My feelings about using AI tools for writing, such as ChatGPT, are this: If you're investing in a college degree (or a master's, Ph.D., or other advanced degree), using AI to write an essay or paper for you defeats the purpose of earning your degree. Writing for yourself is essential for thinking through complex problems and for creative analysis. Outsourcing it to a bot is no different than having someone else do your work. That said, AI can be used in ways that assist and support writing without generating it for you, such as helping with proofreading, editing, etc. (although there is a fine line between the two). We will discuss this topic during the week that we will explore the AI industry’s impact on water use and abuse, and together develop a class code of conduct on acceptable AI use for the rest of the quarter.
