# AI Course Policies

**主要内容**：斯坦福允许教师按课程目标和学科自定 AI 政策。本页说明政策应如何对齐荣誉准则，并列出学术诚信工作组建议的四项结构：使用程度、教学理由、使用示例、引用与披露方式；同时指向 PACE 模板、案例库、Teaching Commons 工作表和 CTL 大纲模板。这些外链资源的正文在 Tool 目录。

At Stanford, instructors may set their own AI course policies, as fits their course learning goals and disciplines. Here are recommended components of a course AI policy and additional resources to help you craft a clear, practical, and pedagogically sound course AI policy.

## Align your course AI policy with Stanford guidance

The Office of Community Standards provides [guidance on generative AI](https://communitystandards.stanford.edu/policies-guidance%23policies-guidance-links/bca-guidance-recommendations#generative-ai-policy-guidance) with respect to the Honor Code. This guidance leaves room for Stanford instructors to set their own standards for AI use in classes and strongly recommends that each course articulate its own specific policy in the syllabus.

## Use a recommended course AI policy structure

The Stanford [Academic Integrity Working Group](https://studentaffairs.stanford.edu/AIWG) recommends the following elements in every course AI policy:

1. Level of use: Is AI use…
 1. Prohibited?
 2. Limited (how and for what)?
 3. Assigned or open?
2. Pedagogical reasoning: For example…
 1. What are your learning goals for the course and how does your AI course policy support them?
 2. In what ways does AI help or hurt student learning?
 3. Why should students follow your proposed learning path?
 4. How will students know if they are using AI effectively or too much?
3. Example use cases: For example…
 1. In your course, can students use AI to obtain feedback or virtual coaching on their learning process?
 2. Can students run simulations related to course content?
 3. Can students use AI to generate additional practice examples or problems?
 4. You could also share guided activities or prompts that promote AI literacy in your field, which you recommend students to use.
4. Directions for citation: Let students know…
 1. How and when students should acknowledge their use of AI in the course.
 2. How and when to confirm and cite sources that back up or verify AI results.
 3. Your recommended or required formats for any statements or disclosures of AI use, and what records students should keep and hand in about their prompts, AI chat transcripts, or other artifacts from their use of AI tools.

## Adopt a clear course AI policy that promotes academic community and engagement

VPUE’s [Promoting Academic Communities and Engagement (PACE) initiative](http://pace.stanford.edu/) recommends strategies for elevating course rigor and energy through practical tools and norms. They have more specific recommendations and examples of [Clear AI Policies](https://pace.stanford.edu/reinvigorate-your-classroom/clear-ai-policies), including a template expanding on what students may and may not use AI for.

## Explore examples of course AI policies from Stanford courses with varying approaches to AI

The [AIMES Library of Examples](https://ctl.stanford.edu/aimes/aimes-library-examples) includes sample course AI policies, along with other teaching artifacts like course learning goals, assignments, assessments, and in-class activities, related to generative AI.

In the **What type of resource** filter, select **Course Policy** to explore further, including filters for approach to AI (e.g., AI-Prohibited, AI-Limited, AI-Assigned), as well as disciplinary area.

Each example includes the course, year and term, instructor, and pedagogy commentary, noting features that you may find helpful when creating your own AI course policy.

## Follow a step-by-step worksheet to create a course AI policy

This Stanford Teaching Commons module on [creating your course policy on AI](https://teachingcommons.stanford.edu/teaching-guides/artificial-intelligence-teaching-guide/creating-your-course-policy-ai) offers a step-by-step process, including a detailed worksheet, to guide you through various considerations. The worksheet checklist format provides an extensive list of possible elements that can give you additional ideas to consider.

## Integrate your AI course policy in your syllabus

The Stanford Center for Teaching and Learning provides a comprehensive [course syllabus template](https://ctl.stanford.edu/faculty-lecturers/course-curriculum/syllabus) that includes sample AI course policy language. One of the samples in the syllabus template describes working with students to co-create AI norms at the beginning of the term; another example in the template prohibits AI use.

The template also includes sample syllabus language on other topics, such as recommended sections addressing accommodations for disabilities, the Stanford Honor Code, and additional course policies, practices and structures. The template is available as a Google Doc for instructors to copy or download and adapt for their own courses.

## Request a consultation on your specific course AI policy

Stanford instructors and TAs are welcome to meet with a Center for Teaching and Learning (CTL) staff member to discuss and gain insights on their specific course and its AI policy. CTL consultants will work with you to understand course learning goals, types of student work, challenges you have encountered, and make evidence-based suggestions based on your approach to teaching. [Request an appointment](https://ctl.stanford.edu/about/appointments)

## Work with your department on shared AI course policies

CTL staff are also available to plan and facilitate custom workshops for instructors and TAs in departments and academic programs. Some departments at Stanford have shared AI policies, or components that are in common across courses or portions of the curriculum. To get started, [request a consultation](https://stanforduniversity.qualtrics.com/jfe/form/SV_0d0KlG0eE8pjjo2) and CTL will reach out to learn more about your goals and setting to start planning a custom workshop.
